# Give quiet responders a way back

Draft for Helen Achebe, Director of Product · Rook Dispatch

## The decision

Approve a prototype showing how Kip recognizes an unexplained quiet period and Meteor Mite regains consideration for appropriate work. Agree the experience before changing production rules.

## Four problems to address

1. **No practical route back:** lower history scores can mean fewer offers, but accepting is the only way to earn points back. Time alone does not help.
2. **Misses count as refusals:** an unanswered offer receives the same penalty as a deliberate decline.
3. **Offers may expire too quickly:** the wait fell from 90 to 60 seconds; actual on-screen time is unknown.
4. **The headline hides unequal opportunities:** acceptance partly recovered while some responders stayed quiet and others got busier. Handlers cannot explain the difference.

Four responders' combined offers fell 83%, from 49 to 8.3 weekly, comparing six full pre-release weeks with three afterward, excluding release week. Missing historical rankings and eligibility prevent a complete causal explanation.

## Kip and Mite's journey

**1. Kip notices.** Mite's card shows one offer in seven days against an earlier average of about eleven; Gale's shows twenty-one. Both separate accepted, declined and missed outcomes. Mite's card says "Fewer offers than usual," not "Routing failure."

**2. They check readiness.** Kip opens "Review availability." Mite can confirm "I'm available" in the existing phone app. Both see "Availability confirmed" and a timestamp only after saving succeeds. Failure shows "Not saved—retry" and retains the previous status. Confirmation never resets history points. "Request review" records a support request; "Pending review" is not a completed repair.

**3. Mite sees a route back.** A recovery view explains that old penalties gradually ease without requiring an acceptance. Show "Recovering," the next adjustment date, and "Adjustment applied" only after a successful update. Missing information shows "Recovery information unavailable." Neither readiness nor recovery guarantees an offer.

**4. An appropriate opportunity arrives.** Demonstrate a qualified, available Mite ranking earlier after recovery, using labelled fictional scores. The phone shows Accept, Decline and time remaining within a proposed 75-second deadline. A confirmed acceptance shows "Assigned" on both views. Expiry shows "Offer expired—moved on" without a decline penalty. A late acceptance cannot claim an already-expired offer. Unknown delivery timing stays unknown.

## Demonstration rules requiring approval

Flag seven-day offers below half the six-week average. Below-neutral scores move halfway toward 0.50 after each 14-day period without a new outcome. Accepting adds 0.08; declining subtracts 0.12; expiry changes no history points. Use a proposed 75-second deadline, adding 15 seconds to the 4.2 window. Delivery delays can still reduce usable time. These are prototype assumptions, not approved policy or reconstructed historical scores.

## Scope and unresolved questions

Build two handler cards, readiness/review actions, a recovery view and offer outcomes, including failed-save states. This tackles recovery, missed-offer penalties and visibility, and tests a longer response window without proving timing adequacy or workload balance. Measure delivery/display/expiry to validate the 75-second choice; verify mandatory skills checks. Exclude a new handler app, Supply changes and guaranteed allocations.
